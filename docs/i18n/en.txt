Analysis scope and output in StatEdu Studio 1.3.0 public release.|Estimation principles, assumptions, formulas, diagnostics and interpretation, expanded from the original Method Notes.|Additions, changes and fixes from the first release to the current version.
scope|Public scope in 1.3.0|CFA, SEM, PLS-SEM and Mediation/Moderation Effects are public analysis menus. Meta-analysis and within-subject treatment repeated-measures ANOVA are excluded from the public installer. Pro is planned for a later version.|This document describes 1.3.0. Distinguish existing mixed repeated-measures analysis from the excluded within-subject treatment analysis. Development records are not the current feature inventory.
data|Data preparation|Import SPSS, SAS, Stata, Excel, CSV and DAT data; check names, labels, measurement levels, categories and reference values. Inspect the sample size and missing-data information for each analysis.|Do not mistake numeric category codes for continuous measurements. Decide missing-data handling and reference categories before analysis; account for sample differences when comparing results.
descriptive|Frequencies, descriptives and crosstabs|Report counts, percentages, location and dispersion statistics and contingency tables. Selected options provide association tests, effect sizes and relevant diagnostics.|Check percentage denominators and treatment of missing values. Examine sparse expected frequencies and independence in crosstabs; distinguish statistical significance from association strength.
group|Group comparisons and ANCOVA|Use independent-samples t tests, ANOVA, ANCOVA and nonparametric group comparisons. Supported options provide variance-sensitive methods, post hoc comparisons and effect sizes.|Examine independence, residual distributions and variances. ANCOVA requires an appropriate covariate–outcome relationship and slopes. Nonparametric tests do not always compare medians alone.
paired|Paired and mixed repeated measures|Use paired, repeated-measures, mixed repeated-measures ANOVA and paired nonparametric analyses. Report time, group and comparison results for the selected design.|Match observations from the same person correctly. Check design-specific assumptions, including sphericity, corrections, interactions and multiple comparisons. The dedicated treatment repeated-measures menu is excluded from the public installer.
correlation|Correlation, reliability and agreement|Use correlation, scale reliability and inter-rater agreement analyses. Select correlation methods and agreement measures appropriate to the variable types and rating design.|Correlation does not establish causation or agreement. High internal consistency alone does not establish unidimensionality. Specify the ICC model, single/average measurements and agreement/consistency definition.
factor|Exploratory factor analysis and PCA|Inspect factor/component counts, extraction, rotation, loadings and explained variance in EFA and PCA. CFA is described separately.|Distinguish the latent-factor purpose of EFA from data reduction in PCA. Consider theory, loadings, factor correlations and diagnostics together; do not overstate confirmation of a structure explored in the same data.
ipa|Importance–performance analysis (IPA)|Open Analysis → IPA and choose direct ratings or derived importance. Pair importance and performance variables in the same attribute order for direct ratings; assign performance variables and overall satisfaction for derived importance. Choose overall, independent groups or matched pre/post. Paired data use corresponding WIDE columns or LONG ID/time variables with two time values. Set reference lines and chart options, run, then inspect sample counts, coordinates, intervals and group/time differences. Save Word/HWPX from collected Results after Add to Results.|Derived importance is a Pearson partial correlation controlling the other performance attributes. Revised IPA log transformation requires positive inputs and retains negative partial-correlation signs. Use common complete cases within groups or a common matched sample across times. Mean differences use Welch or paired t tests and are second minus first. Holm correction covers finite displayed direct-score p values; 95% intervals remain pointwise. Derived importance and differences use percentile bootstrap, requiring at least 80% and 50 valid replicates to display intervals. No t-test p value is attached to partial-correlation differences. The common reference is a respondent-weighted mean of coordinates, not a pooled-sample partial correlation. Split file runs separate analyses; specify groups inside IPA for comparisons.
regression|Regression and hierarchical regression|Use OLS, HC3 robust inference and bootstrap regression. Hierarchical analysis compares successive blocks and explained-variance changes. Selected output includes sr², f², collinearity and residual diagnostics. Hierarchical regression supports up to four blocks. Each step retains the variables from earlier blocks and adds the next block.|Hierarchical comparisons use common complete cases for the final model. Distinguish OLS change tests, HC3 Wald tests and bootstrap ΔR² intervals. Check collinearity, influential observations and residuals; notes explain only displayed statistics.
mediation|Mediation and moderation effects|Assign predictor, outcome, mediator, moderator and covariate roles and draw paths on the canvas to estimate direct, indirect, total and conditional effects. This is not a numbered-model selection workflow. Unsupported path structures are checked before execution.|Assess indirect effects using bootstrap intervals and moderation using interactions and conditional effects. Observational paths alone do not establish causality. Check valid resamples and warnings; withheld inference does not mean nonsignificance.
generalized|Logistic, GLM and penalized regression|Use logistic and generalized linear models, Ridge, LASSO and Elastic Net. Report coefficients and performance with their outcome scale, family, link and cross-validation settings.|Do not interpret an OR as a probability difference. Check family, overdispersion and link suitability. Penalized coefficients differ from ordinary OLS inference; performance measured on the training data is not external predictive performance.
longitudinal|Longitudinal, panel and survey data|Supported designs include GEE, LMM, GLMM, fixed/random-effects and complex-survey analyses. Check subject/cluster identifiers, time, weights, strata and clusters as applicable.|Do not treat repeated observations as independent cases. Distinguish population-average and subject-conditional effects; specify correlation/random-effects structures and design information. Structural equation menus do not support all these designs.
survival|Survival analysis|Use Kaplan–Meier, log-rank, RMST, Cox and supported competing-risk analyses. Check input format and event coding first; unsupported combinations are restricted.|Specify the time origin, units, censoring and event codes. Check Cox proportional hazards and the RMST restriction time. Competing-risk CIF, cause-specific HR and subdistribution SHR are distinct estimands.
cfa|Confirmatory factor analysis (CFA)|Use ML, MLR and ordinal-data WLSMV/DWLS estimation; inspect loadings, fit, reliability, AVE, HTMT and supported measurement-invariance comparisons. Normal/Wishart selection is limited to applicable ML settings.|Match the estimator to the measurement level. Check convergence, negative variances, large factor correlations and local misfit first. Do not assume automatic constraint release for partial invariance or fully automatic parceling.
sem|Structural equation modeling (SEM)|Report measurement/structural paths, fit and supported direct, indirect and conditional effects. Bootstrap defaults to 5,000 resamples; results reflect selected BC or percentile intervals and other settings.|Current structural models cover independent cross-sectional data. Do not interpret them as multilevel, complex-survey or longitudinal SEM. Examine measurement invariance before group path comparisons; multigroup moderated models have support limitations.
pls|PLS-SEM and PLSc|Use reflective Mode A, formative Mode B and reflective PLSc, path/measurement diagnostics and supported prediction/group comparisons. PLS/PLSc Missing-Data Handling uses indicator means via seminr::mean_replacement; each bootstrap resample recomputes means. Bootstrap defaults to 5,000 resamples.|Do not apply PLSc to formative models. Bootstrap inference is restricted when fewer than 80% of whole resamples are valid. Distinguish effect inference from exact-fit bootstrapping; check MICOM/MGA and cross-validation conditions and warnings.
planning|Sample size, power and effect size|Calculate sample size, power and effect size for supported tests. Record alpha, assumed effect, group allocation and test direction with the inputs.|Ground effect assumptions in research evidence. Do not generalize simple-design calculations to adequate sample sizes for clustered, longitudinal or complex structural models. Observed power alone does not explain a nonsignificant result.
reporting|Tables, figures and exports|Public 1.3.0 provides HTML/image and PDF/Word/Excel exports. HTML/PDF reports for Mediation/Moderation Effects, CFA, SEM and PLS-SEM append the result model figure. Images preserve the displayed result layout; Free uses 300 dpi and development/Pro uses 600 dpi. Review current results and use Add to Results to collect them. HTML, PDF, Word and Excel saving is supported. HWPX is available only in the accumulated Results screen with Korean UI and is written directly, without Word or Hancom conversion. Word/HWPX offer main tables, appendix tables, explanations and figures; main tables are selected by default. Saving uses captured displayed results without rerunning analyses. Free figures use 300 dpi and developer figures use 600 dpi. HTML includes a cover and linked table list.|Save transparent backgrounds, with white where transparency is unsupported. Valid p ≥ .05 paths are dashed when nonsignificance display is enabled; paths without p are distinct. Covers follow the UI language; Free shows the logo and StatEdu, Institute of Statistics. Check table orientation, wrapping, notes and paired residual plots after export.
validation|Validation and reporting boundaries|Comparisons include general analyses, regression, longitudinal and survival analyses against SPSS; CFA/SEM against AMOS; and PLS-SEM/PLSc and CB-SEM against SmartPLS. These are cumulative validations incorporated into 1.3.0. The Validation page summarizes conditions and remaining differences.|Agreement on selected indices does not establish equivalence for every analysis, dataset or inference. Match data, coding, estimator, missingness and tolerances. For example, 49 of 50 comparable AMOS models agreed; 41 of 2,895 SPSS LMM values differed. Detailed source data and internal execution records are not part of the public documentation.
